# Chat design

This document owns how the dashboard's frame and main chat area look: the backdrop and the main card, the chat header, messages, Work groups and tool rows, approval and question cards, and the composer. The side panel's contents are owned by [side-panel-design.md](side-panel-design.md); behaviour (sending, history, approvals, compaction) by [dashboard.md](dashboard.md) and [dashboard-composer-design.md](dashboard-composer-design.md); tool display hints by [tool-ux-design.md](tool-ux-design.md).

The approved mockups are kept outside the repository: they were drawn with private sample content. They are the visual reference (sizes, padding, radii and colours are taken from their CSS) and are named here by view: `chat-dark`, `chat-light`, `work`, `question`, `composer`, `narrow`. The gallery (§9), captured from a neutral stress session, shows the result.

## 1. Why it changed

The chat looked like a stack of forms next to a side panel that had been redesigned:

- **Rules everywhere.** Full-height rules divided the rail, the session list, the chat and the side panel, and full-width rules divided the toolbar, the header and the tab strip, so the screen read as a grid of boxes rather than one place to work.
- **Two header rows.** A 58 px toolbar (breadcrumb, search, Activity) sat over a header with a 20 px title, the full temporary path and the agent.
- **Heavy messages.** Each user message was a bordered card labelled "You"; every tool call was another bordered box; panel receipts looked like more tools.
- **Raw output.** Terminal previews showed the server's `[stdout]`, `[stderr]` and `[exit N · t]` markers; "Saved preview" and "Arguments" were bare links.
- **Repetition.** A pending `ask_user` question appeared three times (a tool row waiting for its result, the question, and "Waiting for your answer"); context usage appeared twice under the composer.
- **Code ligatures** turned `!==` and `<=` into single symbols.

## 2. Visual language

The chat uses the side panel's language ([side-panel-design.md](side-panel-design.md) §2): the bundled OFL fonts Inter and JetBrains Mono only, chips with tinted backgrounds and a word for every status, cards with a 1 px border and 10 px radius, monospace only for commands, paths and code, with ligatures off. Chat prose is 15 px at line height 1.65; secondary text 11 to 12.5 px muted.

## 3. Frame

There are no full-height or full-width rules. Regions are separated by tone and by one rounded card (the `chat-dark` and `chat-light` mockups):

- **Backdrop.** The app shell has a backdrop darker than the canvas (`--backdrop`: `#07060b` dark, `#eeecf3` light) with 8 px padding at the top and bottom. The rail, the workspace sidebar (workspace button, New chat, search, sessions) and the side panel sit directly on it: no background of their own and no border.
- **The card.** The main area is the only card: the canvas colour, a 1 px `--frame` border (`#211b2d` dark, `#e4e1ea` light), a 14 px radius, and in the light theme a soft shadow (`0 1px 2px rgba(20,12,40,.05), 0 4px 14px rgba(20,12,40,.04)`). On a chat it holds the header, the conversation and the composer; on other pages it holds the toolbar and the page. The side panel is outside the card, so the chat sits between two matching sides; with the side panel closed the card reaches the right padding.
- **Spacing.** The sidebar's content and the side panel's content are each 16 px from the card.
- **One centre line.** The workspace button (52 px high, its top on the card's top edge), the chat header (52 px), the side panel's title row (52 px) and the rail's logo share one centre line, 35 px below the top of the shell.
- **Selection on the backdrop.** The hover tint is too close to the light backdrop, so the selected session row and the active rail item use the canvas colour with a 1 px shadow in the light theme; the search field and the workspace button keep their border.
- **Second column.** On Agents, Library and Settings the sidebar lists rows that behave like session rows: the `--backdrop-hover` fill on hover, never an underline, and the current row raised like the selected session (its icon in the accent colour). Library rows show an icon and how many items each page lists; a count that is loading or failed is left out rather than shown as 0. Agent rows show an initial avatar, a muted line with the model and what the agent selects (or its package), and a dot on the default agent; a long name ends in an ellipsis on one line. Only the label or agent name is the link's accessible name.
- **Side panel tabs** are a segmented control: a strip with a 10 px radius and 3 px padding (dark `--seg`; light translucent white with a 1 px `--frame` ring), tabs with a 7 px radius, the selected tab raised on `--seg-on` with a small shadow. No underline and no rule under the strip. Dragging, keyboard movement, overflow scrolling and badges are unchanged.
- **Resizing.** The sidebar and side panel resize handles sit in the gaps beside the card, invisible until hovered or focused (an accent line).
- **Narrow windows.** Below 900 px the sidebar is the drawer it was and the side panel the modal it was, both in the backdrop colour. Below 600 px the frame is full-bleed: no backdrop padding, the card has no radius or border (the `narrow` mockup), and the horizontal rail keeps its contents and layout on the backdrop colour without a bottom rule; the change of tone separates it from the chat.

## 4. Header

On a chat the app toolbar is not shown: the chat header is the card's first row and carries the toolbar's controls (the `chat-dark`, `chat-light` and `narrow` mockups). Other pages, and `/chat` before a session is open, keep the toolbar inside the card.

- **One row,** 52 px high, 18 px left and 14 px right padding, no rule under it. Its centre is the frame's centre line (§3).
- **Left to right:** "Workspace menu" (below 900 px only, opens the drawer); the session title (15 px, weight 600, one line with an ellipsis, the whole title in its tooltip); the agent as a muted chip; the workspace path (12 px muted, `~/…` under the home directory once the workspace listing reports it, otherwise the full path; the full path in its tooltip); then icon buttons with an 8 px radius, 32 px (the mockup's 30 px raised to the dashboard's minimum target size): Quick navigation (⌘K), Activity (warning colour while an approval waits, the count of running operations as a badge), Side panel (tinted while the side panel is open, `aria-expanded`) and More actions (agent, rename, Copy workspace path, delete).
- **Below 600 px** the agent chip, the path and Quick navigation are hidden; the path stays in the title row's More actions → Copy workspace path, and ⌘K still opens Quick navigation.
- **Width.** The conversation column and the composer are 720 px at most with 28 px at the sides (14 px below 600 px), centred in the card.

## 5. Messages

- **Your messages** are bubbles on the right of the column (`--bubble`: `#1e1930` dark, `#f1eff6` light), 78 % of the column at most, an 18 px radius, 10 px by 15 px padding, line height 1.55. Attachments sit inside the bubble above the text. There is no "You" label: the time and **Copy message** sit under the bubble and appear on hover or keyboard focus (always on touch screens).
- **A message on its way** is the same bubble the moment you press Send, with a muted 12.5 px status line under it instead of the time: **Sending…** until the dashboard has a receipt, then **Waiting for the agent** until the agent commits the message, when the history's bubble takes its place. The status comes from the session's operations, so a reload shows it again.
- **A message the agent did not get** keeps its bubble with an error-toned line under it: **Not delivered · the agent did not receive this message** and the reason (the operation's error, or "Stopped before the agent received it"), with **Retry** and **Edit**. It shows when the latest turn's operation ended before the agent committed your message: a hook denied it, the runtime failed, or Stop came while it started. A request the server refused is **Not sent** with its error, and its text and attachments go back in the message box; one whose receipt is not confirmed yet is **Not confirmed**. Retry sends the text, the file references and the images again as a new request (images are uploaded again); Edit puts them back in the message box, before anything typed there. While the bubble says how its operation ended, that operation's run status and the Preparing line are not shown again. After a reload in the same tab Retry and Edit keep the text and the file references, and the bubble lists each image to attach again.
- **The assistant's messages** start with a 20 px "r" mark on the accent tint, **Raw** and the time (12.5 px), then the answer as prose, then **Copy message** under it. "Incomplete response" stays under an interrupted message.
- **Prose** is the Chat font size (15 px by default) at line height 1.65; paragraphs 10 px apart; headings at weight 650 (19, 17 and 15 px for levels 1 to 3); lists indented 20 px; inline code in the Code font size (13 px by default) on a tint with a 5 px radius.
- **Tables** are one box with a 1 px border and a 10 px radius that scrolls sideways when it is wide: muted 12 px headers at weight 600, 13.5 px cells, a rule under each row but the last and no inner vertical rules.
- **Code blocks** have a 10 px radius and a header row with the language (11.5 px, muted) and a **Copy** button (named "Copy code" for assistive technology); the code is in the Code font size at line height 1.6.
- **Ligatures are off for all code** in the dashboard, so `!==` and `<=` read as typed.
- **Scope.** This typography, the table box and the code header belong to chat messages. Markdown elsewhere (a Library preview, a side panel markdown block) keeps its look; only the ligature rule is global.
- **Sizes.** The Chat font size and Code font size settings default to 15 px and 13 px; a size you chose is kept.

## 6. Work

- **The summary line.** A turn's reasoning and tool calls form one group with a 13 px summary line: a chevron, **Worked 0.3s** (weight 550; **Working** with a spinner at the right end while the turn runs), `· 4 tools` muted (`· Reasoning` when there are no calls), a `1 failed` error chip when calls failed (an exit other than 0, a timeout, a denial, a cancellation, an error, an unknown outcome), and a `Needs approval` warning chip while a call waits. At the right, one pill per panel the group updated opens that panel in the side panel: Files changed as `+4 −1`, the lines this group's file changes added and removed (the panel's own summary counts the whole session); a checklist as `Todo 1/3` from its latest update; another panel as its latest title; a rejected update as an error pill with the panel's name.
- **Opening.** A group opens when you toggle it, or by the **Show reasoning** and **Show tool details** preferences. It does not open by itself while it runs, when a call fails or when a call waits for approval: the summary line carries those states, and the approval card follows the group (§7). Your toggle is kept per group.
- **Rows** sit on a 1 px rule, 16 px in. Reasoning is a muted italic line with a brain glyph that ends in an ellipsis and opens to the full text. Each call is one row, 30 px high, with an 8 px radius and a hover tint: a 14 px status glyph (check, x, spinner, shield for approval, ban for denied or cancelled, clock for timed out) whose state is also accessible text, the title (13 px, weight 550), the summary (12 px monospace, muted, ellipsis), then at the right the lines added and removed by a file change, a pill per other panel the call updated (`1 of 3 done ↗`), a chip only for states other than OK (`Exit 1`, `Timed out`, `Denied`), the duration (11.5 px) and a chevron. At 390 px the summary is hidden so the row stays one line.
- **Cards.** Opening a row shows a card under it (10 px radius, the side panel's card colour, an error border for a failed call). Failed calls start open, and every call does with **Show tool details**. A command's output is on the code background (12 px, line height 1.6, at most 16 lines before it scrolls): the `[stdout]`/`[stderr]` markers are removed, stderr lines are in the error colour, and the footer says `stderr · exit 1 · 0.1 s`; the channel is left out when a hidden-lines notice hides where the visible lines came from. A diff uses the side panel's colours, without its file headers; the footer names the file. Other results keep their bodies. The footer has **Copy** (output or diff), **Full output** when a full copy was saved, and **Arguments**, which opens the exact arguments above the footer. A display abbreviation, a tool-truncated result and a result code show as muted footer text. A running command shows no body but a line linking to Commands, where its live output is.
- **Receipts.** A call's panel updates are pills on its row and in the summary line, never rows of their own. An update without a call in the group (an action you ran from a panel, tagged *You*) is a pill row of its own.

## 7. Approval and questions

- **Approval card.** A call waiting for approval is a card after its group, not inside it, so it shows while the group is closed. It has the side panel's card colour, a 12 px radius, 12 × 14 px padding and the warning border. Its top line is a `Needs approval` warning chip with a shield, **Review** (which opens the side panel's Approvals tab, where the intended files and their proposed content are readable in full — [side-panel-design.md](side-panel-design.md) §3.3), and, at the right, the tool name and when the request expires (`write_file · expires 14:32`; the full date is its tooltip). The headline is the call's icon, its title and its summary in monospace, computed from the normalized arguments ([tool-ux-design.md](tool-ux-design.md) §4.4), and for a write, an edit or a patch the number of lines it adds (`+3`: the content, the new text or the patch's added lines). When an argument holds multi-line text (a file's content, a replacement), the longest such text follows in a code-coloured preview of at most six lines, the last an ellipsis when it is longer; a one-line command has no preview because the summary shows it. The actions are **Arguments**, which opens the exact arguments and the intended effects, then **Deny** and the primary **Allow once** at the right. Allow once answers only this call and does not change your rules. The card does not take focus when it appears, and it leaves when the call is answered; the call's row then shows its result (`Denied` with the ban glyph).
- **The row of a waiting call.** While it waits, the call's row has the shield glyph and a `Needs approval` chip, and the group's summary line has the warning chip.
- **Questions.** A question (`ask_user`, or a plugin's form placed in the chat) shows once: as the question card after its group, with its status chip, options and **Send answer**. The call's row in the group says `Waiting for you` in an accent chip while the question waits and is OK once it is answered; its card has only **Arguments**, because the question card holds the question and the answer. A call that asks several questions in turn waits for you while any of them is pending. History records without a call id are recognised by the tool identity `builtin/ask_user`; a call that failed before it asked keeps its error in its card, since no question card explains it.
- **Working status.** The line above the composer appears only for preparing, compacting, and a question that waits outside the chat. While tools run, the group's **Working** says so; while an approval or a question waits, its card does. Screen readers still hear every state through the live region.

## 8. Composer

The composer is a card under the conversation, in the same 720 px column with 28 px at the sides and 16 px under it (the `composer` and `narrow` mockups).

- **The card.** A 1 px border, a 16 px radius, the side panel's card colour, 12 px padding at the top and right, 10 px at the bottom and 16 px at the left, and an accent border while something in it has focus. The message box is 15 px, as chat prose. Attachment chips and the command list keep their places above the box.
- **The toolbar,** left to right: **Add attachment** (`+`, a round 32 px button with a border), the request pill (`Reasoning: default` or `Reasoning: high` with a sparkles icon; its accessible name lists every setting, as before), a spacer, the context button, then **Send** or **Stop**. Send is a round 32 px button in the text colour with an arrow, at 35 % opacity while it is disabled; Stop has the same shape with a square. While an operation you started here runs, the button is Stop when the message box is empty and Send when it holds a message.
- **Sending while the agent works.** Send (Enter, or Ctrl/⌘ Enter with that preference) then queues the message: it reaches the agent after the current step. A round 32 px chevron with a border follows Send and opens a menu with **Interrupt and send** (`Ctrl/⌘ Shift Enter`), which stops the current step and starts a turn with the message. The hint says so while the agent works: `Enter to send after the current step · Ctrl/⌘ Shift Enter to interrupt and send`.
- **Waiting messages** are cards above the composer card, oldest first, in its width: a 1 px border, a 12 px radius, the card colour; the text in 13 px clamped to two lines; one line under it with a chip for where the message is (**Sends after the current step**, **Sending…**, **Paused** after Stop or an error, **Blocked: reason** when a hook refused it, **Already sent** when the agent took it before your action) and its attachments; and three 32 px icon buttons: **Edit** (the message, its files and images go back into the message box, before what is typed there with a blank line between), **Delete**, and **Send now** (interrupt and send this message). Up in an empty message box edits the last card. After three cards the list scrolls, at most 24 % of the window's height, so the conversation keeps at least half of it. A delivered message leaves its card and shows as your bubble where the agent got it, between the Work groups of the turn; the turn's time then shows on its last group only.
- **The context button** is an 18 px ring and the percent of the context used, against the auto-compact trigger when the agent sets one and otherwise the model's window. The ring is the accent colour, amber from 80 % and red from 95 %. Its accessible name is `Context: 71% used` (`Context: not measured yet` before the first turn).
- **The Context card.** The button opens a 250 px card above it: `Context` and the percent chip; the tokens in large type, `~5.9k / 8.2k tokens` (without `~` when the provider reported them), followed by `until auto compact` when the limit is the trigger; a 6 px bar in the ring's colour; one line with the messages kept and the trigger (`30 messages kept · auto compact at 7.4k`, or `auto compact off`), ending in `· last measured` when the measurement is older than the conversation. **Compact now** summarizes the model context, as `/compact` and Details' Compact now do; while it cannot run (the session is loading, no agent, work is running) it is disabled and the reason is written under it. **Details** opens the side panel on its Details tab. Before the first turn the card says "Measured after the first turn." and keeps Compact now.
- **No footer.** Context usage is only in the button and its card; there is no line of text under the composer.
- **The hint** under the card, centred in 11.5 px muted text, says how to send: `Enter to send · Shift Enter for a new line · / for commands`, or `Ctrl/⌘ Enter to send · Enter for a new line · / for commands` with the Ctrl/Cmd-Enter preference. The message box refers to it for screen readers.
- **At 390 px** the card is 12 px from the sides and the hint is read by screen readers only.

## 9. Verification

- **Stress session.** `tests/dashboard-ui/chat-stress.ts` runs a real-length session through the mock provider: a long Vietnamese request, reasoning, a todo, a successful command, an edit with a diff, a failing `npm run lint`, an answer with a heading, a table, inline code, a code block with `!==` and `<=`, and a list; a second turn whose write waits for approval; a third with a pending `ask_user` question.
- **Geometry.** `tests/dashboard-ui/chat-layout.spec.ts` asserts the contract of each section at 1440 px (side panel open and closed), 800 px and 390 px, in both themes, plus axe.
- **Gallery.** After `npm run build`, `npx tsx tests/dashboard-ui/capture-chat.ts` writes `test-results/chat/chat-<view>-<scheme>.png` (views `desktop`, `work`, `queued`, `composer`, `question`, `narrow` and `queued-narrow`); `npx tsx tests/dashboard-ui/compare-chat.ts` reads the local mockups (`RAW_CHAT_MOCKUPS`, default `test-results/chat-mock/`; it does nothing when they are absent) and writes `test-results/chat/compare-<mockup>-<scheme>.png`, the mockup on the left and the dashboard on the right at the same CSS size. They are compared by eye, not as pixel assertions.
- **Performance.** A long session is measured, not judged by eye: §10.

## 10. Performance

A long chat stays responsive: typing never waits for the timeline, and a streaming answer never makes the composer lag.

- **The draft.** The message box's text lives in a small store per session, over the map that keeps drafts when you switch chats (`web/src/composer/draft-store.ts`). Only the composer subscribes to it, so a keystroke re-renders the composer and nothing else. Sending, checking a receipt, inserting `@path` and a panel's prompt action read and write the store; their rules are unchanged.
- **Turns.** The timeline is split at your messages into turns. Every turn renders through the same memoized component, under a key that survives what can happen to it: an answer arriving, the next message, and an earlier page of history adding records to its start. A Work group's key survives the same way, so an open group, an open tool card or an answer you are typing stays as it is. A turn whose records, tool calls, approvals, questions, views and panels did not change is not rendered again; while an answer streams, only the last turn renders.
- **Stream batching.** Stream events are queued as they arrive and applied together once per animation frame, in arrival order, as a transition, so a keystroke is handled before them. A hidden tab runs no frames, so a timer applies the queue within 100 ms. A snapshot (a reconnect) applies the queue first and then itself at once, so the chat never shows a mix of the two. The reconnect position advances as each event arrives.
- **Off-screen entries.** Messages and Work groups that are direct entries of the conversation use `content-visibility: auto`, so an entry outside the viewport costs no layout or paint. `contain-intrinsic-size: auto 160px` holds an estimate until an entry first renders, then its last rendered size, so the scrollbar and the scroll position stay steady. The entries stay in the DOM, so find in page and a selection that spans them reach them. Chrome leaves a skipped entry's content out of the accessibility tree, so a screen reader reaches only the entries near the viewport. Their paint is clipped 16 px outside their box (`overflow-clip-margin`): the message actions sit 8 px left of the message, and their focus ring 5 px further. Approval cards, questions and inline tool views are not skipped: they are few, interactive and usually at the bottom. Following and Jump to latest scroll again on the next frames while the content height still changes, at most three times, because an entry that renders for the first time replaces its estimate; while following, the view also stays at the bottom whenever the content grows later (a late entry, font or image). Only scrolling up stops following; content that grows below the view does not.
- **Earlier history.** A loader row sits above the first entry while older history remains. When it comes within 600 px of the top of the viewport the next page loads, one page at a time, and after each page it checks again, so a short window keeps loading until it fills or the history ends. The row is a status that says "Loading earlier messages…" while a page loads. A failed page shows its error and **Retry** in the row and stops loading on scroll until Retry. The first entry in view when the page arrives keeps its offset from the top of the viewport: from the commit that adds the page, and again on the next frames while entries that render for the first time replace their size estimate. Scrolling while the page loads is not undone, and Jump to latest cancels the correction. Following does not resume.

- **Stress session.** `tests/dashboard-ui/chat-long.ts` runs the first turn of the chat stress session (§9) once through the mock provider. It then copies that turn's history records 300 times into a new session, with fresh segment, turn, operation and tool call ids, so every copy is a distinct turn; the request and the answer's heading end with the turn's number, `[lượt N]`. Each turn has a request, reasoning, a todo, commands, an edit with a diff, a failed lint and an answer with a table and a code block. A live turn then streams a long Markdown answer in 2400 small frames, 10 ms apart. The stress agent's auto compaction is off, and the script checks that the live turn made exactly one provider request.
- **Measuring.** After `npm run build`, run `npx tsx tests/dashboard-ui/measure-chat.ts --label <name>`.
  - The script opens the session at 1440 × 900 with the side panel open, loads the whole history, types while idle, starts the live turn, and types while it streams. Then it scrolls from the bottom to the top.
  - It writes `test-results/chat-perf/report-<name>.json` and prints a table.
  - `--cpu 4` slows the CPU four times through the DevTools protocol; that run only reports.
- **What it measures.**
  - **Keypress to paint.** One sample per keystroke. Keys are typed through the DevTools protocol, one character at a time. A keystroke's sample is the longest Event Timing duration among its `keydown`, `keypress` and `input` events, which runs from the key press to the next paint. Event Timing reports only durations of 16 ms or more, so a keystroke without an entry counts as under 16 ms, but only when the observation is known to be complete. That means the observer is attached and its entries are drained, and the calibration passed.
  - **Calibration.** Each run starts with 5 keystrokes against a listener that blocks for 80 ms. Each must measure at least 80 ms, so a probe that sees nothing cannot pass.
  - **Overlap.** Typing counts as "while streaming" only if the answer grew while the keys were typed and the turn was still running after the last one.
  - **Long animation frames** over 50 ms: their count and total while typing idle, while typing during the stream, and while scrolling. The script finds its own elements with CSS selectors: a role query computes accessible names over the whole chat and would itself make a long frame.
  - **Re-rendered nodes.** A test-only React DevTools hook counts the DOM nodes whose props changed in each commit. It counts them in three regions: the conversation, the conversation before the last message you sent, and the side panel. The script installs the hook; the dashboard ships nothing for it.
  - **History.** The time to load the whole history.
- **Budgets.** `--assert` fails the run when a budget is missed:
  - the 95th percentile of keypress to paint is under 50 ms, both while idle and while streaming, at 300 turns;
  - typing changes no node in the conversation or the side panel;
  - while an answer streams, no node before the last message you sent changes;
  - no long animation frame over 100 ms while streaming. `--strict-frames` lowers this to 50 ms; this applies once off-screen messages skip rendering.
- **Validity.** Whatever the flags, a run fails as invalid when the calibration fails, a keystroke has no sample without complete observation, or the typing did not overlap the stream.
